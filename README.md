# duet: can a machine find new ways for two operations to work together?

> **Work in progress.** A research log, not a finished result. No theory found so far is claimed to be new.
> [`REPORT.md`](REPORT.md) gives every number with its caveats and the command behind it.

Many structures in algebra are two operations held together by a few laws. In a lattice, two absorption laws force
`x · x = x`, though neither law does alone. That is *synergy*. duet runs an open-ended search over small theories in
two operations, `x · y` and `x ∘ y`, looking for fusions of this kind that nobody has named. Every claim is checked by
theorem provers and finite models, and no language model takes part in the search.

**What is new, and what is not.** Machine search for theories is old: AM and HR, then Wolfram's enumerations of axiom
systems. So are exhaustive catalogues of one-operation varieties, from Ježek and Kepka to the ETP and its successors.
A literature check found no earlier open-ended search over *sets* of laws in *two* operations with synergy as the
target, no taxonomy of the theories such a search finds, and no conjectures stated across those families. That
combination is what duet adds ([`notes/metafield.md`](notes/metafield.md)). The families are the first step. The plan
is to catalogue patterns across families next, then conjectures, and perhaps theorems.

**New to the subject?** [`intro/intro.pdf`](intro/intro.pdf) explains it from sets and functions, in 10 pages. Exercises
with solutions are in [`intro/exercises.pdf`](intro/exercises.pdf).

## The story, in short

- **[How it started](docs/history.md).** A genetic search over theories, first in the cloud on two cores. Its first
  "find" was two of the four laws of McKenzie's 1970 lattice basis. Since then most of the work has gone into telling
  rediscovery from novelty.
- **[Why](docs/motivation.md).** Synergy, bridges between two theories, and the jazz aim: a fusion that works on its
  own terms but is not clearly connected to either parent. The explored share of the space is tiny, but the search
  grows like a coral: outward from known mathematics, run by run, and away from the corner our checks recognise, while
  the human literature stays far larger.
- **[The evolutionary search](docs/search.md).** MAP-Elites over 71,369 laws, with streaming verification by E,
  Vampire, Mace4 and Lean. Each run taught a lesson:
  - it rediscovers known structure;
  - its best candidates were disguises;
  - it learned a trick about squares;
  - steering off that trick led to Steiner loops in disguise;
  - screening that disguise moved the top candidates off classical ground (X6);
  - skipping everything already known sent the verifiers to new ground: 12 of X7's top 20 carry every automatic
    mark;
  - the search went back to the Steiner corner (X10), so the disguise screen moved into the search itself (X11);
  - with it, the top candidates left 𝔽₄: 10 of X11's 20 are disconnected, and two lead the ranking;
  - pressure toward rare families (X12, against a control arm) doubled the families at equal size and kept the
    scores, but did not give better candidates by our automatic marks.
- **[The ranking](docs/ranking.md).** A preregistered novelty ranking, N1, with its live table.
- **[Families and emerging patterns](docs/families.md).** Every verified theory sorted into a family, with each
  run's share, the families and laws that are new or emerging, and an accumulation curve.
- **[Candidates studied closely](docs/candidates.md).** Twelve so far. Two turned out to be known: a fragment of
  Heyting algebras, and X12's top theory `71fd2cc6`, a commutative g-dimonoid (a 2014 notion) with one more law. Ten
  are unidentified. The two to show an expert first:
  - `55cb2c91`: ∘ is a partial swap of ·, and with · = ∘ only one-element models are left;
  - `3818b88f`: a triple system with a selector operation, which turns out to be nearly free. X7's `de0c0cf3` is
    the same idea with one law fewer.
- **[Open problems](docs/open-problems.md).** S12, still open after 2-hour prover runs; non-implications that need
  infinite countermodels; a conjunction of two ETP laws.
- **[How this was made](docs/how-made.md).** Mostly by an AI coding assistant, directed by the author. No LLM is in
  the scoring loop.
- **[Running it, and where to look](docs/running.md).**

## Where it stands (27 Nov 2026)

- **Latest:** X12 (preregistered), the first run since T4 with a control arm. Arm `fusion6`, which adds pressure toward
  rare families, ran 8 hours beside `fusion5`, 2 searchers and 2 verifiers each. Six of eight predictions held.
  - At equal sample size, fusion6's verified theories span twice as many families (131 against 63), and 69 of its
    families are new to every earlier run (fusion5: 4). There are now 241 families.
  - The open question was whether that diversity costs quality. It did not: 55.6% of fusion6's verified theories have a
    positive score, against 47.5%, and its best score is the highest of either arm.
  - It did not give better candidates: 3 of its 20 are disconnected against 8, and 4 reach level 7 against 6.
  - Its price is the rescore, 38% of searcher time on an archive 13 times larger. The preregistered bound was 15%.
  - Level 0 passed 200M (201.6M), level 7 is 79, level 8 is still 9.
- **Found after the run:** a verifier bug. z3 proofs had a resource limit but no wall-time limit, and some ran for
  hours without reaching it. Two of fusion5's verifiers spent their last 5 to 7 hours on one proof each, and fusion6
  lost about 4.6 hours to one task that crashed its verifiers again and again. So the two arms got unequal verifier
  time (deviation 13). Verifier V8 caps every z3 proof in time.
- **Since then:** X11's top candidate `1f430506` was studied and is not identified, so level 8 is now 10. Its two laws
  force every square to be a left identity of ·, and ∘ is only a commutative choice of values that · constrains.
- **Before that:** X11 moved the disguise screen into the search, and found that the searchers stop pushing once the
  verified scores rise: the verifiers are the bottleneck.
- **Next:**
  - V8 in the next run, and a cheaper rescore for `fusion6` (a sample of the archive, or less often as it grows);
  - a push gate that follows the queue, or fewer searchers and more verifiers;
  - a specialist's eye on the unidentified candidates, starting with `55cb2c91` and `3818b88f`.
