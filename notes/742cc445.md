T = { (A1) x·(y∘(x∘z)) = x, (A2) x∘((x∘y)·(z∘x)) = x }.

## Structure (E)

- **A1 is McKenzie's lattice law L1.** It is identical as a law, and it holds in the 2-element lattice under both
  readings. **A2 is not a lattice law:** it fails there under both readings (part 3).
- **Emergent laws.** T proves the absorption law x·(x∘y) = x, x·(y∘x) = x, x·x = x, x∘x = x, x∘(x∘y) = x,
  (x∘y)·x = x∘y, x·(y∘y) = x·y and x∘(y·y) = x∘y. **Each needs both axioms:** neither A1 nor A2 alone proves any of
  them (part 2).
- **Not proved** (E, 30 s each): the other absorption x∘(x·y) = x, McKenzie's L2–L4, and commutativity or
  associativity of either operation.
- The reading C = {x·(x∘y) = x, x·(y∘x) = x, x∘(x∘y) = x, (x∘y)·x = x∘y} does not recover A1 or A2 (E, 60 s). T is
  stronger than its short consequences.
- **∘ is never associative in a non-degenerate model.** T plus associativity of ∘ proves x∘y = x and x·y = x∘y (E;
  the argument was suggested by the literature agent and checked by machine). So T excludes skew lattices,
  quasilattices and paralattices, which are all built on bands.
- **Shadow:** with x·y = x∘y, T is the left-zero band. The two laws become ETP Equations 54 and 828 (mapping checked
  by `law_index`). ETP names neither.
