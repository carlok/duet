## Structure (E)

  associativity of · is short: law 3 with ∘ commutative gives x·(y·z) = y·(x·z), and with commutativity that is
  associativity.
- **Both associativities are emergent** (part 5). E saturates on associativity of · from each single law. For ∘ it
  saturates from laws 1 and 2. Law 3 alone holds with · constant and x∘y = 1 − x on {0, 1}, which is not associative
  (by hand). In the diagonal survey of T4–X3 (`notes/diagonal.py`) no scored theory proved emergent associativity;
  later runs were not surveyed for it.
- **Plain reading, E both ways.** T is equivalent to K = { · a commutative semigroup, x∘y = y∘x, (x∘y)·z = (x·y)·z,
  x∘(x·y) = x∘y }.
- **A symmetric reading, E both ways.** T is equivalent to K3: two commutative semigroups on one set, each unable to
  tell the other's products from its own one level down, plus one law of ∘ alone:
  - x·(y∘z) = x·(y·z) and x∘(y·z) = x∘(y∘z);
  - x∘(x∘y) = x∘y.

  The last law is independent of the rest (Mace4, 2 elements). So is x·(x·y) = x·(y·y), which T proves.
- **Further consequences (E):** (x·y)∘z = (x∘y)∘z, x∘y = (x·y)∘(x·y), x∘y = (x·y)∘y, and (x∘y)∘(x∘y) = x∘y: every
  ∘-product is ∘-idempotent.
- **Not implied** (E runs out of resources; finite models refute them): x·x = x, x∘x = x, x·x = x∘x, x·y = x∘y,
  x·(x·x) = x·x (a 3-element model), and the middle dimonoid axiom, here (x∘y)·z = x∘(y·z). The checks script calls
  it D3, counting the two associativities as D1 and D5.
- **Dimonoids and g-dimonoids.** In Gavrylkiv's numbering a dimonoid has two associative operations with
  (D1) (x⊣y)⊣z = x⊣(y⊢z), (D2) (x⊢y)⊣z = x⊢(y⊣z) and (D3) (x⊣y)⊢z = x⊢(y⊢z); a g-dimonoid drops D2, and it is

## The models, in general

Call a and b **twins**, a ρ b, if a·z = b·z for every z: ρ is the kernel of the regular representation of (S, ·).

**Theorem.** The models of T are exactly the pairs (S, τ) such that:
1. (S, ·) is a commutative semigroup with x·x·y·z = x·y·z;
2. τ picks one element in every ρ-class that contains a product;
3. x∘y = τ(the ρ-class of x·y).

*Proof, T ⇒ (S, τ).* E gives (x∘y)·z = (x·y)·z, so x∘y ρ x·y.
- E gives x∘y = (x·y)∘y. So if a ρ b then a∘c = (a·c)∘c = (b·c)∘c = b∘c: ∘ cannot tell twins apart.
- E gives x∘y = (x·y)∘(x·y). So if x·y ρ u·v, then x∘y = p∘p = q∘p = q∘q = u∘v, with p = x·y, q = u·v, using
  twin-blindness twice and commutativity.
- So x∘y is a function of the ρ-class of x·y, and its value lies in that class. That function is τ.
- Finally, x∘(x·y) = x∘y gives (x·x·y)·z = (x∘(x·y))·z = (x∘y)·z = x·y·z, which is condition 1.

*Proof, (S, τ) ⇒ T.* Law 1 holds. Law 3: (x∘y)·z = τ([x·y])·z = x·y·z = y·(z·x). Law 2: x∘(x·y) = τ([x·x·y]) and
y∘x = τ([x·y]), and condition 1 says x·x·y ρ x·y. ∎

Condition 1 implies x·x·y = x·y·y: put z = y, then swap x and y. That is the third proved claim.

In every model x∘y is a function of the class of x·y, and for every semigroup either every choice τ gives a model or
none does, as the theorem says.

A non-degenerate model needs twins: a commutative semigroup whose regular representation is not faithful. The smallest
example has 3 elements. Take · with 0 a zero, 1·1 = 0, 1·2 = 0 and 2·2 = 2. Then 1 is a twin of 0, and τ picks 1:
x∘y = 1, except 2∘2 = 2.

**The shadow.** With x·y = x∘y, the models are the commutative semigroups with x·x·y = x·y, which is τ = the identity.

## Reading

T is a commutative semigroup in which squares are invisible to products of length three, together with a free choice of
one representative in each twin class of products. As with `de0c0cf3` and `3818b88f`, ∘ is "the structure of ·, plus a
free choice". There it was a choice on the blocks of a triple system, here a choice of twins. That is a pattern across
families: the search keeps finding a second operation that is a selector on classes defined by the first.
- The synergy is real but shallow: associativity of both operations and the absorption laws follow in a few steps.
- Why it reached level 7: it has no classical model among the probes, because a non-degenerate model needs twins and
  a monoid has none (a·1 = b·1 gives a = b), while most of the probes' semigroups are monoids. Its synergy is off the
  diagonal, and none of our checks knows g-dimonoids.
- What identified it was the literature, not a check: T is a commutative g-dimonoid with one more law. The search found
  a recent research topic (Gavrylkiv's counts are from July 2026) from three short laws, with no knowledge of it.
- The theorem may still be worth a line to an expert: it describes every model of this subvariety as a commutative
  semigroup plus a transversal of its twin classes.
