## Reading

T splits Stein's law x(xy) = yx across two operations. B makes ∘ almost a right projection on products. The fusion is
real, since neither law gives x·(x∘(y·x)) = x, but shallow: two rewriting steps. Its one-operation shadow collapses.
Not identified. A natural next question: does a Stein quasigroup (·), with ∘ chosen so that A holds, ever satisfy B?
Stein quasigroups are idempotent, and T does not force x·x = x.
