# Candidate `a9295908`: what the theory says (22 Nov 2026, evening)

Found in T4's prior arm; N1 rank 3 (the novelty ranking, PREREGISTRATION.md N1). Status: **candidate**, not a result.
Checks: `notes/a9295908_checks.py` (E, Vampire, Mace4, `law_vector`), and `lean/Duet/A9295908.lean` (general proofs,
plus one `decide`).

T = { x·(x∘x) = x,  (x∘y)·y = y,  x·y = x∘(y·x) }.

## 1. The theory, read plainly

**T is equivalent to C** (E both ways; Lean `T_iff_C`, by hand):

- · is commutative and idempotent: x·x = x, x·y = y·x;
- x∘(x·y) = x·y;
- (x∘y)·y = y.

T also implies x∘x = x (E; Lean `T_diag`).

The hand proof is short. Law 3 with y := x∘x, together with laws 1 and 2 at (x, x), gives x∘x = x, so · is
idempotent. For commutativity, put a = x·y and b = y·x. Laws 2 and 3 give a·b = b and b·a = a. Law 3 at (a, b) then
gives b = a∘a = a.

**· need not be associative.** E and Vampire saturate on associativity, and Mace4 finds a 3-element model: · is a
non-associative commutative idempotent table and ∘ is the right projection (Lean `not_assoc`, by `decide`).

**The ·-parts of T's models are exactly the commutative idempotent groupoids.** Every model of T has one, by C.
Conversely, any commutative idempotent groupoid with x∘y := y (the right projection) satisfies C. So T says nothing
more about · than "commutative and idempotent". The synergy is in how the three bridge laws force commutativity and
idempotence, which no single law implies: that is the off-diagonal emergent law that put it in N1's top 3.

## 2. When · is a semilattice

Read y ≤ x as x·y = y. When · is the meet of a semilattice, T says exactly (Lean `inf_iff`):

- y ≤ x ⇒ x∘y = y: each map y ↦ x∘y is the identity on the down-set of x;
- y ≤ x∘y: it never moves an element down.

So for each x, the map y ↦ x∘y fixes ↓x and is inflationary. Nothing else is required.

**A model on every meet-semilattice with a top:** x∘y = y if y ≤ x, else ⊤ (Lean `threshold_model`). On a chain this
is Gödel implication changed on the diagonal: x∘x = x instead of ⊤. That is the 3-element model the search found, and
the family on chains of 3 to 6 elements seen this afternoon. Gödel or Heyting implication itself is not a model:
for y ≤ x it gives x→y, not y. The threshold operation is a model on the Boolean algebras of 4 and 8 elements too
(`law_vector`; part 4 of the checks).

## 4. Literature, and an identification

The earlier, broad check (22 Nov, web-only agent) found nothing. The targeted check (22 Nov evening, web-only agent;
its key claims rechecked here) found two things.

**On chains, ∘ is a known connective.** It is the "strict order" of Gödel logic:

- A ≺ B := (A⊃B) ∧ ((B⊃A)⊃A) in M. Baaz, A. Ciabattoni and R. Zach, "Quantified propositional Gödel logics", LPAR
  2000, LNCS 1955, 240–256, doi:10.1007/3-540-44404-1_16, arXiv:math/0006122, §2;
- written (B→A)→B in Baaz, Preining and Zach, Ann. Pure Appl. Logic 147 (2007);
- also in Baaz and Gamsakhurdia, arXiv:2407.16683 (2024).

The agent read only the cited passages. Those sources use the connective as an abbreviation for strict order; none
studies it algebraically.

**Every Heyting algebra is a model.** With · = ∧ and x∘y = (y→x)→y, Heyting algebras satisfy T:

- y ≤ x gives y→x = ⊤, so (y→x)→y = y;
- y ≤ (y→x)→y always.

Lean proves it for every generalized Heyting algebra (`heyting_model`). The second term from the literature,
(x→y)∧((y→x)→x), gives models too. Both were checked on 7 finite Heyting algebras: chains of 3–5 elements, the
Boolean algebras 2² and 2³, 2×3, and 1+2². On chains the two terms equal our threshold operation (part 6 of the
checks). So **T is valid in all Heyting algebras under a term interpretation**: it is a fragment of the theory of
Heyting algebras, written in ∧ and the strict-order connective, without associativity.

No source was found for the theory T itself, for its semilattice reading, or for the threshold operation on
non-chains. ∘ cannot be the implication of Hilbert, BCK, Heyting, subresiduated or implication algebras: those all
have x→x constant, while T forces x∘x = x.

## 5. Assessment

The dig identifies the candidate as a weakening of known structure, the way weakly associative lattices weaken
lattices:

- **Its ∘** is, on chains, a known connective of Gödel logic.
- **Its full models** include every Heyting algebra, by a term.
- **Its ·-reducts** are exactly the commutative idempotent groupoids.

What remains is the fact that three bridge laws force commutativity and idempotence. Specialists may also want a
precise description of the variety: for example, whether T plus associativity of · is exactly the equational theory
of Heyting algebras (or of Brouwerian semilattices) in the language {∧, ≺}. That question is open here.
