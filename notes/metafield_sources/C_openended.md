## What looks covered by this strand, and what looks uncovered

**Covered.**
- **(a) The framing has been claimed before.** Wolfram has said since 2002 that one should enumerate axiom systems from the simplest and ask where known mathematics appears. He found a Boolean single axiom this way. Since 2021 he calls the systematic study of what rule systems do "ruliology", a new science that starts out like natural history. Machine theory formation in finite algebras, with a prover, a model finder and an interestingness-driven search, has existed since HR (1999). HR's ancestor AM dates from 1976. Lenia (2019) gives a full natural history (taxonomy, families, nomenclature) of objects found by evolutionary search in a mathematical system. Experimental mathematics, Davies et al. and murmurations state and demonstrate the arc from data to pattern, conjecture and theorem. Davis's critique warns that the "new paradigm" framing tends to be overstated. "A new (meta)field" is therefore not defensible as stated. At most it is a new instance or combination.
- **(c) The statistics are established.** Richness estimators applied to the outputs of a discovery process (STADS, reachable coverage, capture–recapture for defects, local-optima counts, Thomson configurations) are an established method. So are its caveats: adaptive sampling violates the estimators' assumptions, reliability is unproven without ground truth, and Chao1 is only a lower bound. The project should cite STADS and Hernando et al. at minimum, and adopt their caveats.
- **(d) Machine conjecturing over classes of objects is old.** AutoGraphiX, Graffiti (cited by HR) and HR do it, and Colton et al. (2004) machine-verified classification theorems. Conjecturing over families is covered for objects, though not for theories.

**Uncovered (nothing found in this strand).**
- **(b)** Quality-diversity or MAP-Elites search, or any open-ended evolutionary search, whose individuals are *sets of equational laws*, in two operations, scored by synergy (laws the whole implies that no single axiom does). What exists is:
  - exhaustive enumeration: Wolfram's single-axiom searches; Le Floch's 456 semigroup theories, which is one operation and must be checked against the other strand;
  - search over concepts and conjectures inside one theory: HR, Minimo, QuickSpec;
  - search over objects or programs toward an objective, often LLM-based: Wagner, PatternBoost, FunSearch, AlphaEvolve.
- **(c)** Richness estimators (Chao1, accumulation curves) applied to *families of theories or varieties*, or to any taxonomy of mathematical theories. The nearest cases count objects (Thomson configurations, local optima) or test-coverage "species", not kinds of theory.
- **(d)** Conjectures stated across families of varieties produced by a search.

The defensible novelty is this specific combination, not a field.
