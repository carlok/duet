## What this strand covers, and what it leaves uncovered

**Covered: the project cannot claim these as new.**
- The arc from catalogue to pattern, conjecture and proof is old and well documented: BSD (1965), Graffiti (1986–), murmurations (2022, with proofs in 2023–2026), Davies et al. (2021). The project can cite it as precedent. It is not a new method.
- Systematic exploration of axiom-system space has been done, together with a theory × model and theory × theorem table and the idea that each axiom system is a potential field. Wolfram did it for one operation in NKS (2002) and Metamathematics (2022), and he has a name for the activity ("ruliology", 2021/2026).
- Exhaustive classification of varieties cut out by short laws, with an ATP plus a finite-model finder, is an established line:
  - the Prague school (Ježek–Kepka, 1979–89);
  - Phillips–Vojtěchovský (2005);
  - Berman–Burris (1996).
- That line is very active right after the ETP: Le Floch's 990 quasigroup laws and 456 semigroup varieties (both with conjunctions), SemiBase's 505 certified semigroup varieties, and Berlioz–Melliès's latent space of theories built from satisfaction statistics on finite magmas.
- Interestingness-driven conjecture and classification over finite algebras with Otter and MACE is HR's programme (Colton, from 1999).
- Ecological richness estimators have been applied to automated discovery (STADS) and to catalogue completeness (Chao1 on chant).

**Uncovered, as far as this strand found.**
- An open-ended, non-exhaustive quality-diversity search (MAP-Elites) over *sets* of laws in *two* binary operations, steered toward bridge laws.
- Synergy (consequences that no single axiom gives) used as a search objective.
- A family taxonomy of verified theories built from the one-operation reduct properties of each operation.
- Chao1 and accumulation curves applied to *families of equational theories*.
- Every exhaustive catalogue found here works in one operation (magmas, semigroups), or in a quasigroup signature derived from one operation, or with model-driven semiring catalogues. None searches two independent operations.

**Verdict.** "A new (meta)field" does not hold: that ground is occupied by ruliology and empirical metamathematics, computational universal algebra, automated theory formation, and the ETP line. A defensible claim is narrower: the *combination* of open-ended search over two-operation law sets, synergy as fitness, and richness-estimated family taxonomy appears new. The same claim should say that exhaustive conjunction catalogues are advancing fast next door (Le Floch; order-≤3 magma conjunctions reported as nearly done).
