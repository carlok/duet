# Strategies for legitimate, probably unexplored, valid new theories (22 Nov 2026)

The aim (the user, 22 Nov): theories in the spirit of the "jazz" example, an original jump of the kind humans make,
not a small step from something known. Our working reading: two existing traditions fuse into a third whose
defining features neither parent had. For us that is two one-operation theories and a bridge, with emergent structure
about how the operations interact.

What the data says so far:
- The searches touched 4,222 of about 10²⁶ law sets, and the duality prior keeps them in the most-studied corner
  (`notes/coverage.py`).
- 97% of the scored theories prove a one-variable emergent law, and none proves emergent associativity
  (`notes/diagonal.py`). The score has been measuring one kind of synergy: what a fusion forces on x·x and x∘x.

| # | strategy | what it targets | status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Off-diagonal gain**: count only emergent laws that no single law gives even with all one-variable consequences | the diagonal attractor; synergy about how the two operations interact | X4 (arm `fusion`, V5) |
| 2 | **Leave the mapped corner**: no duality prior; skip theories valid in the 2-element lattice | the region humans studied most | X4 |
| 3 | **Fuse traditions**: proposals made of one restrictive ·-law, one restrictive ∘-law and a bridge | the "jump": combining two traditions that humans rarely put together | X4 |
| 4 | **Model novelty**: prefer theories whose small models are not affine, group-derived, lattices or rings | theories that classical constructions don't explain | idea; first as a post hoc feature |
| 5 | **Spectrum as a signal**: sparse or arithmetic spectra (as for Steiner quasigroups or central groupoids, n = k²) | what makes combinatorialists care | post hoc on survivors (Mace4 + FMB to 8–10) |
| 6 | **Strength of emergent laws**: weight an emergent law by what it implies on its own | strong emergents (associativity, quasigroup laws) over weak ones | alternative to 1, not run |
| 7 | **Bigger language**: order-5 laws, a unary operation (T7) | room beyond what is enumerated | later; count and estimate the RAM first |

Gates that keep a find legitimate and valid. Each is already in the pipeline unless marked otherwise:
- **Legitimate:**
  - non-degenerate models, and not definitional;
  - V4's split and division screens;
  - `soup.relabel`, `soup.hidden`, `soup.dedupe`, and the R1 catalogue (post hoc).
- **Valid:**
  - every emergent law has a prover proof;
  - every non-implication has a model: finite, a certified saturation, or a model on ℤ;
  - Lean certificates for the finite models.
- **Probably unexplored:**
  - literature checks across fields: Latin squares, quasigroups, Yang–Baxter, racks, cycle sets, bisemilattices, ETP;
  - then a check by someone who knows the field. Our own searches are weak evidence: they have already missed one
    source.
