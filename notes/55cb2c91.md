# Candidate `55cb2c91`: what the theory says (22 Nov 2026, evening)

Found by the early runs; N1 rank 1. Status: **candidate**, not a result.
Checks: `notes/55cb2c91_checks.py` (output `.out`). Lean: `lean/Duet/T55cb2c91.lean` (general proofs).

T = { x·(x∘y) = x,  x·y = ((y·z)·x)∘x }.

## 1. The theory, read plainly

**T is equivalent to C** (E both ways, 60 s per law; Lean `C_implies_T` for one direction):

- **(A)** x·(y·z) = x·y. The product x·(y·z) ignores z: composing two left translations gives a constant map.
- **(B)** x·(x∘y) = x. Each x is a value of its own left translation, reached at x∘y.
- **(C)** x·y = (y·x)∘x. ∘ recovers x·y from y·x and x.

E does not derive any of (A), (B), (C) from the other two in 30 s, so none is known to be redundant;
(x·y)·y = x·y was in a first version of C and turned out redundant. T also implies (x·y)·y = x·y (each right
translation is idempotent), x·(x·y) = x·x and x·(y·x) = x·y (part 1: 30 short consequences proved, 13 of them
emergent).

## 2. Which · can occur, and what ∘ then is

**A groupoid (A, ·) is the ·-part of a model iff** (Lean `reduct_iff`, for every type):

1. x·(y·z) = x·y;
2. (x·y)·y = x·y;
3. every x equals x·y for some y;
4. y·x = y'·x implies x·y = x·y'.

Conditions 3 and 4 are not equations, so the ·-parts do not form a variety.

**What ∘ is.** Given such a ·, ∘ is **forced** on pairs of the form (y·x, x): there it is the swap y·x ↦ x·y, which
condition 4 makes well defined. Elsewhere ∘ is **free**, as long as u∘w lands on some w with u·w = u.

On 3 elements, 63 · tables satisfy condition 1, and the prediction (conditions 1–4) matches the brute-force answer
(does some ∘ give a model?) on all 63. 19 of them extend to a model (part 3).

The first prediction left out condition 2 and failed on 6 tables. The missing piece is that the forced values must
satisfy (B) at u = y·x, which needs (y·x)·x = y·x.

## 3. Models

Non-degenerate models up to isomorphism (`soup.profile`): 0 on 2 elements, 5 on 3, at least 365 on 4, at least 2,099
on 5 (both capped).

The left-zero band x·y = x satisfies conditions 1–4. But it forces ∘ to be the right projection, so it is degenerate.
The smallest non-degenerate model has two left zeros, 0 and 2, and 1·0 = 1·1 = 0, 1·2 = 1.

## 5. Assessment

The dig turns the two opaque laws into a transparent structure:

- **·** has constant double left translations and idempotent right translations;
- **∘** is a partial swap y·x ↦ x·y, completed freely by any element that its left translation sends back to itself.

The synergy is real but modest. The two laws together force the right translations of · to be idempotent, and they
make the swap well defined. Neither law does either alone.
