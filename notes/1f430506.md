## (x·x)·y = y by hand, from E's 9-clause proof

1. A twice: (x∘y)·((y·z)∘w) = w∘(y·z). Take A with x := y·z and y := y∘x; its left factor (y·z)·(y∘x) is x∘y by A.
2. By 1, z∘(z·w) = s·s with s = (z·w)∘z: it is a square. B with that square gives ((x∘y)·(z∘(z·w)))·x = x.
3. Rewrite the left factor by 1, taking z := y·c. So for every product p, (p·w)∘p is a left identity.
4. A left identity ℓ gives y = ℓ·y, so every element is a product. Hence (y·w)∘y is a left identity for all y, w.
5. B with x := a·b and y := a: the factor ((a·b)∘a)·(t·t) is t·t by 4. So (t·t)·(a·b) = a·b.
6. Every element is a product (step 4), so (t·t)·y = y.

Commutativity then takes three more steps.
7. For a square u, A with x := u gives y·(u∘z) = z∘u for every y. With y := u this gives u∘z = z∘u. So r = u∘z is a
   right zero: y·r = r for all y.
8. r·r = r, so r is a square, and so a left identity.
9. A with y := r: (x·r)·(x∘z) = r·(x∘z) = x∘z, and A says this is z∘x.

## The models, in general

**Theorem.** Let (S, ·) be a magma and U its set of squares. For x, y in S let

W(x,y) = { w : p·w = w for every p in x·S ∪ y·S, and (w·u)·x = x and (w·u)·y = y for every u in U }.

The models of T are exactly the pairs (·, ∘) with ∘ commutative and x∘y ∈ W(x,y) for all x, y.

*Proof.* T proves commutativity. With it, A says that x∘z is fixed by every left translation by an element of x·S,
and, with x and z swapped, of z·S. B says that (x∘y)·u fixes x, and y, from the left for every square u. Each law
constrains one value of ∘ at a time, so these membership conditions are A and B. ∎

Checked on every magma with n ≤ 3: the formula ∏_{x≤y} |W(x,y)| gives 12 labelled models on 2 elements and 1,491 on
3, and Mace4 gives the same (labelled counts from its 6 and 259 isomorphism classes; part 2).

**Corollaries.**
- **The ·-reducts** are exactly the magmas with (x·x)·y = y that have a right zero.
  - Necessity: E, and step 7 (for a square u, u∘u is a right zero).
  - Sufficiency: under (x·x)·y = y a right zero r is a square (r·r = r), so a left identity. Then r lies in every
    W(x,y), since p·r = r and (r·u)·x = u·x = x. So the constant x∘y = r gives a model.
  - Checked on every magma with n ≤ 3: no magma is a reduct without both properties, or has both without being one

- **The one-operation laws of · that T implies are exactly the consequences of (x·x)·y = y.** Any magma with that law

## Reading

T couples two very weak one-operation theories: a magma whose squares are left identities, and a commutative magma.
The coupling asks only two things of each value x∘y.
- It must be fixed by the left translations from x·S and y·S.
- It must turn the squares into left translations that fix x and y.

The synergy is (x·x)·y = y. Neither law gives it alone, and once it holds, commutativity of ∘ follows in three steps.

The pattern is a recurring one among the studied candidates: ∘ is a constrained choice, not an operation with laws of
its own.
- `71fd2cc6`: a choice of twins.
- `de0c0cf3`: an idempotent ∘ on a TSQ, with 65,536 choices of ∘ over the 16 labelled TSQs on 4 elements.
- `3818b88f`: a selector on a triple system.
