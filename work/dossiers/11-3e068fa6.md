## Literature check

**Half of the Δ-bijective core, plus idempotence.** E's convergent presentation has 3 rules: (x∘y)·(y∘x) = y, x·x = x and x∘x = x. The first says that (x,y) ↦ (x∘y, y∘x) has the left inverse (u,v) ↦ (v·u, u·v). So ∘ is Δ-injective, with · as the transposed decoder. In a finite model, injective means bijective, so every finite model also satisfies (y·x)∘(x·y) = x: it is a Δ-bijective pair (Bonatto, Kinyon, Stanovský and Vojtěchovský, arXiv:1910.02148, Lemma 2.10), relabelled, with both operations idempotent. That law is not an equational consequence: the presentation is convergent and does not join its two sides, so some infinite model fails it. `10c98c4b`, relabelled (rev∘, rev·, and either with swap), implies this theory (E). A finite-versus-infinite gap of the same kind as the one-sided quasigroup laws (CLAUDE.md, facts confirmed). Not split; implies no named theory under the 8 relabellings.
