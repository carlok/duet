## Literature check

**Split, so not a fusion** (`soup.split`, E). Bijective translations turn both bridges into one-operation laws: law 1 makes every left translation of · a bijection, and law 2 does the same for the right translations of ∘. E proves both directions of the equivalence with V(·) plus V^op(∘), where V = {x·(x·y) = y, (x·y)·y = y} (ETP 16 and 28) and V^op = {(x∘y)∘y = x, x∘(x∘y) = x}.

Nearest literature: bikei (involutory biquandles; Nelson and Rosenfield, arXiv:1603.02719, Definition 1). Their laws (ii.i) (x b y) b y = x and (ii.ii) (x a y) a y = x also make translations involutions. The candidate is not a bikei fragment: their Example 1 bikei (x a y = x b y = σ(x), σ an involution) fails x·(x·y) = y under either assignment of the operations.
