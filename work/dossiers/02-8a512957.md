## Literature check

**An idempotent left quasigroup in disguise.** E proves both directions of the equivalence with {x·(y∘x) = y, (x·y)∘x = y, x·x = x, (x·y)·y = y, x·(y·x) = y·x}. So y∘x is the left division x\y of ·, and the theory is the variety of idempotent left quasigroups (·, \) satisfying x·(y·x) = y·x (ETP 333) and (x·y)·y = y (ETP 28). `soup.relabel` finds the same thing, up to relabelling, against the named left quasigroups. Rank 9 (`977c3603`) is the same without (x·y)·y = y.

Not a rack: E saturates against left distributivity. It is also not a twisted Ward left quasigroup ((x·y)·(x·z) = (y·y)·(y·z); Stanovský and Vojtěchovský, arXiv:2002.02854) and not a cycle set ((x·y)·(x·z) = (y·x)·(y·z), Rump 2005). The 3-element model is still a quandle: L₀ = L₁ = id, L₂ = (0 1). The affine models x·y = a·x + b·y with a² = 0 and b = (1+a)⁻¹, for instance 2x + 3y on ℤ/4, are Alexander quandles. Left quasigroups as a variety, with their commutator theory: Bonatto, *Nilpotent left quasigroups*, arXiv:2204.04448. Searches (22 Nov 2026) found no source that names left quasigroups with x(yx) = yx.

Verdict: a subvariety of a named variety (left quasigroups), cut out by two one-operation laws. It is a candidate for a name only if those two identities matter in the left-quasigroup or Yang–Baxter literature, and we found no sign that they do.
