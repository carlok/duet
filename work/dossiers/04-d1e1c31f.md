## Literature check

**An absorption-type algebra without associativity.** It implies the absorption pair with ∘ reversed, x·(y∘x) = x and (x·y)∘x = x (`soup.relabel`, E). E's convergent presentation (10 rules) gives both operations idempotent. · alone satisfies x·(y·x) = x (ETP 10) and (x·y)·y = x·y (ETP 378), and ∘ alone satisfies (x∘y)∘x = x (ETP 25) and x∘(x∘y) = x∘y (ETP 323). The bridges are x·(y∘x) = x, (x·y)∘x = x, (x∘y)·y = x∘y and x∘(x·y) = x·y. E finds no implication either way, under any of the 8 relabellings, with `1ca933c1`, `3078949b`, `87137d88`, `2dc27508` or `69dfbd8e`. Not split (`soup.split` found a finite certificate). E saturates against associativity and commutativity of either operation, and against (x·y)·z = x·z and x·(y·z) = x·z (and the same for ∘). With both operations associative, E proves that each is a rectangular band, so the associative models are pairs of rectangular bands. With · commutative the theory is trivial: x·(y·x) = x and (x·y)·y = x·y then give x·y = y, and so x = y (hand proof). That rules out commutative hosts such as weakly associative lattices and bisemilattices. According to a delegated lookup of Leech's *Noncommutative Lattices* (2020, pp. 29–32), x·(y∘x) = x and (x·y)∘x = x are two of Leech's skew*-lattice absorption laws, and his noncommutative lattices are always associative. A bisemilattice “co-absorption law” of the same shape as (x∘y)·y = x∘y appears in Godowski, Demonstratio Math. 25 (1992) (review only). No source found for the theory (22 Nov 2026: web, zbMATH, the lookup). It reads as a nonassociative generalisation of pairs of rectangular bands.
